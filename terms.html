Using Vigil within the law

Vigil records pictures, and sound if you switch it on. Where you point a camera, and what you do with the recordings, is your responsibility.

  • UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. If a camera covers anything beyond your own boundary, such as a pavement, a shared path or a neighbour's property, data protection law is likely to apply to you. The Information Commissioner's Office has guidance for home CCTV at ico.org.uk.
  • Protection from Harassment Act 1997. Using a camera to watch or follow someone can be harassment.
  • Sexual Offences Act 2003, section 67 (voyeurism). Recording people in places where they expect privacy, such as a bathroom or bedroom, is a crime.
  • At work. Recording staff or the public means meeting the duties an organisation has under data protection law, including clear signs.

Laws differ in other countries. If you are not in the UK, the rules where you are apply, and you are responsible for following them. Before downloading from this site you are asked to confirm: “I understand and will use this app within the law”.

Using a camera lawfully

You are responsible for where your cameras point and for what you do with the recordings. In particular:

  • Use Vigil only on property you own or control, or where the person who does has agreed.
  • Tell the people who live with you, and visitors where appropriate, that a camera is in use. Do not use Vigil to watch or listen to anyone secretly.
  • Do not point a camera at places where people expect privacy, such as bathrooms or another person's bedroom, or into a neighbour's home or garden.
  • If a camera covers anything beyond your own boundary, such as a pavement, a shared path or a neighbour's property, data protection law is likely to apply to you. In the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office publishes guidance for people with home CCTV at ico.org.uk.
  • If you use Vigil at a place of work, you must meet the duties an organisation has when it records people, including telling them clearly with signs.
  • Recording sound is more intrusive than recording pictures. Leave sound switched off for a camera unless you need it.

Privacy masks, per-camera sound control and the printable sign in the app are there to help you do this.
