Privacy policy

Last updated 15 November 2026. This policy covers the Vigil app for Android (package uk.jj.vigil) and this website. Vigil is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom. It also covers the web viewer on this site.

The short version

  • Vigil has no accounts and no servers of its own. The developer never receives your video, your sound, your alerts or anything from your Google account.
  • There is no advertising, no analytics and no tracking in the app.
  • Recordings are stored on the camera phone and in your own Google Drive. Live video goes directly between your own devices.
  • Everything Vigil detects is worked out on the camera phone. Nothing is uploaded to be analysed.

What stays on your phones

The camera phone uses its camera and microphone only while you have asked it to watch. Android shows its own indicator whenever they are in use. The camera phone keeps:

  • a rolling recording, which overwrites its oldest footage when the space you allow is used up;
  • event clips and their thumbnail pictures, until they have been copied to your Google Drive and the space is needed;
  • pictures taken once a minute for the daily time-lapse, until that day's film has been made and copied to your Drive;
  • your camera settings, including any zones and privacy masks you draw, and, if you record one, your own spoken warning;
  • anything you enter for the optional extras: a PIN (stored only in a scrambled form), an encryption passphrase, a stream password, a webhook address and an AI key. These stay on that phone and are not copied to Drive.

Movement, people, pets, vehicles and sounds are recognised by software running on the camera phone. The picture and sound are not sent anywhere to be analysed.

The viewer phone keeps your settings, the cameras it knows about and, for a while, copies of recordings you have played. If you set a home for arming by location, Android on the viewer phone watches for you leaving and arriving. The location stays on the phone; the only thing passed on, through a small file in your Drive, is whether that phone is "home" or "away".

Vigil asks you to sign in to Google so that it can save clips to your Google Drive. It asks for one permission.

How it is used

Only to provide the features above. Google does not give Vigil access to any file in your Drive that Vigil did not create, and Vigil does not ask for it.

Where it is stored

Clips and the other files are stored in your own Google Drive, and copies are kept on your own phones as described above. Your email address is stored on your phone only.

Who it is shared with

Nobody, unless you choose to. Google user data is not sent to the developer, not sold, not used for advertising, not used to train any AI or machine-learning model, and not seen by any person other than you.

Vigil does not share anything in your Drive with other Google accounts. A household shares its cameras by signing each phone in to the same Google account, and each phone is then given a role, full control or view only. If you turn on the optional AI helper described in the next section, one still picture from each event is sent to the AI service you choose; that picture comes from the camera phone, not from your Drive.

Vigil's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

People you record

A camera records whoever is in front of it. You decide where a camera points and you are responsible for using it lawfully and fairly towards the people it records, including your household, visitors, neighbours and anyone at a place of work.

Vigil gives you tools to record less: privacy masks that black out areas so they are never recorded, a choice for each camera of whether sound is recorded, the option to pause cameras while you are home, and a sign you can print. The developer has no access to your recordings and cannot view, pass on or delete them for anyone.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Clips in your Google Drive are deleted automatically once they are older than the period you set, which is 30 days unless you change it. Clips you star in Drive are kept.
  • You can delete any clip yourself, in the app or in Google Drive. Deleting the Vigil folder in your Drive removes everything the app has stored there.
  • Data on a phone is kept until you remove it. Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling Vigil deletes all of it from that phone.
  • You can withdraw Vigil's access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of Vigil holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. Your data is on your own phones and in your own Google account, where you control it directly using the steps above. If you believe you have been recorded by someone else's Vigil camera, the person who runs that camera holds the recording, not the developer. You have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

Every connection the app makes to Google uses HTTPS, and live video between your devices is encrypted. The app's settings are held in private storage that other apps cannot read, and nothing from Vigil is included in Android's automatic backups. You can add a fingerprint lock to the viewer and a PIN to the camera phone, and you can choose to encrypt a camera's recordings with a passphrase before they are saved to Drive. Roles for phones are kept by the app itself: anyone who knows the Google account's password can still open what is in its Drive, so share that password only with people you would trust with the recordings.

This website

Apart from the web viewer, this site sets no cookies, runs no scripts or analytics and loads nothing from other websites.

The web viewer is a page that runs in your own browser. It loads Google's sign-in script from accounts.google.com, asks you to sign in with the same single Drive permission as the app, and then talks directly to Google Drive and, for live view, directly to your camera phone. It keeps your sign-in and any encryption passphrase in the page's memory only; closing the page forgets them. It sets no cookies of its own, though Google may set its own in its sign-in window. Nothing you do in the web viewer is sent to the developer.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub, as the host, receives the internet address of visitors and handles it under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The app file will be downloaded from GitHub as well, on the same terms.

Children

Vigil is a tool for adults to look after their own home and property. It is not directed at children and the developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Changes

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy, this page will be updated and the date at the top changed.

Contact

For a question about this policy or about your data, open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public, so please do not include personal details in one.
